Words on this site

Glossary

Short definitions in the sense used here. A dotted word on other pages is the first use of that term and links back to this list. Hover or focus it for the short form.

The longer account of the disease is on About SCA3.

ASO

Antisense oligonucleotide. A short synthetic strand of nucleic acid designed to bind a specific RNA. In SCA3, ASO drugs are being studied as a way to change how much faulty ataxin-3 message a cell uses. Binding the RNA is the idea.

CAG repeat

A run of the DNA letters cytosine, adenine, and guanine. Everyone has some CAG repeats in ATXN3. In SCA3 the run is expanded. GeneReviews summarizes rough ranges: normal alleles about 12 to 44, intermediate alleles beginning around 45, and full-penetrance disease alleles often cited from about 60 to 87. Laboratories do not all use the identical cutoffs.

Anticipation

The tendency of an unstable repeat to grow when it is passed from parent to child, which can bring symptoms earlier in the next generation. In SCA3 the repeat more often grows than shrinks. Expansions are somewhat more likely when the expanded allele comes from the father, but that is not a rule for any one child, and the age of onset still cannot be predicted precisely from the repeat length.

SARA

Scale for the Assessment and Rating of Ataxia. A clinician scores gait, stance, sitting, speech, and limb coordination. A higher score means more ataxia. Trials often use a SARA range as an entry rule, for example mild to moderate disease. The score describes the signs that day.

Premanifest

A person who carries a disease-causing expansion and does not yet have ataxia. Papers also say presymptomatic or pre-ataxic. “At risk” is broader: it includes relatives who do not yet know whether they inherited the expansion.

Intrathecal

Given into the cerebrospinal fluid, usually by a lumbar puncture. VO659 and S233107 are described as intrathecal drugs. That is how those studies give the drug.

PGT-M

Preimplantation genetic testing for a monogenic (single-gene) condition. Embryos created by IVF can be tested for the familial ATXN3 expansion, and an embryo without it can be transferred. It is an option for a future pregnancy. It does not change the genetic status of a child who is already born.

Biomarker

A measurable sign used to track a disease or a drug’s effect: a score, a brain-scan measure, or a protein in blood or spinal fluid.

NfL

Neurofilament light chain, a protein released when nerve fibers are injured and measured in blood or spinal fluid. In the READISCA cohort, higher plasma NfL was one of the differences between SCA3 carriers who later developed ataxia and those who did not. It is studied as a marker.

Natural history study

A study that follows people over time without assigning a drug, to record how the disease changes. Clinical scores, scans, and fluid samples from those studies are what later trials use to plan sample size and to choose measures. CRC-SCA is the main network of this kind in the United States and Canada.

Autosomal dominant

One copy of the expanded gene is enough to cause the disease. The gene is on a non-sex chromosome, so sons and daughters have the same chance. Each child of a carrier has about a 50% chance of inheriting the expansion.

Predictive testing

A gene test in a person who has no symptoms of the disease, to learn whether they inherited the expansion. In adults it is usually done with counseling before and after the result. For children without symptoms, professional guidance is generally to wait. See For families at risk.

Penetrance

The chance that a person with a given genotype will develop the disease. GeneReviews treats a range of longer ATXN3 repeats as full penetrance, meaning the disease is expected, and notes that the lower edge of that range is not perfectly sharp. An intermediate allele is a shorter expansion that can be unstable when passed on.

SCA3

Spinocerebellar ataxia type 3, also called Machado-Joseph disease. A progressive ataxia caused by an expanded CAG repeat in ATXN3, the gene that makes ataxin-3.

Machado-Joseph disease

The older name for SCA3, from the Machado and Joseph families described in the 1970s. It is the same disease, not a separate one.

Ataxin-3

The protein made from ATXN3. An expanded CAG repeat changes the protein. It is also the name of this site.
