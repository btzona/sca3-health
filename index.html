If SCA3 is part of your family's story, you're not alone, and you're in the right place.

Why this site exists

I have two daughters. Their mother died of SCA3 at 42, and each of them has a 50/50 chance of inheriting it. I built this site for them first, so they'll always have a clear, honest picture of where the research stands. It's also for every family living with SCA3 and everyone working to end it.

— A father

There is no approved treatment yet that stops or slows SCA3.

As of the latest refresh, no medicine has been approved anywhere to change the course of the disease. Doctors can treat many of the symptoms, and physical therapy can help people stay active longer.

What's being tested now

Many research teams and companies are working on it. This site follows every step.
