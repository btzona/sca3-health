Therapies

Pipeline

Where the programs in the 5 October 2026 review sat. “Investigational” means a program is being studied. It does not mean it works.

Not medical advice. This is a snapshot, last reviewed 5 October 2026. It does not change itself when a headline appears. Studies that are enrolling are on Open trials. The longer account, with the same sources, is on Where things stand.

Last reviewed 5 October 2026

Still in the lab

Cure Rare Disease ASO

An experimental genetic drug, still in the lab. The company received a U.S. orphan-drug designation in May 2025. No study in people with SCA3 had been posted when this page was reviewed.

Not something a person can join. It is not in the clinic.

There isn’t a public trial listing to check yet.

AAV / RNAi and other gene therapy

Ideas for gene therapy that would quiet the SCA3 gene. Still in the lab, not in a study of people, as of this page.

Not something a person can join yet.

There isn’t one public trial listing for this group of ideas.

Early studies in people

VO659 (Vico)

An experimental drug given by spinal injection, aimed at the expanded genetic repeat that causes SCA3. The same study also includes SCA1 and Huntington disease. It is an early safety study in adults.

When this page was reviewed it was enrolling in Europe. NCT05822908. Ages 25–60, with mild to moderate ataxia. In February 2026 the company said a U.S. study had been cleared. That is the company’s update, not a regulator’s approval.

Not for someone who has no symptoms yet.

The latest study status will appear here.

S233107 (Servier)

An experimental drug given into the spinal fluid, meant to lower the harmful ataxin-3 protein. It was an early study, comparing the drug with a placebo, in adults who already have symptoms.

On 5 October 2026 this card said the study had not started enrolling. Read again on 17 October 2026, the public European page says the trial ended on 11 September 2026. Denmark, France, Spain, and Portugal are each marked ended, because the company decided to stop. The page does not show that anyone was recruited. It still lists a planned start of 1 September 2026 and a planned end of 31 October 2030. Servier’s own page still said “not yet recruiting” on 17 October 2026. The two pages disagree. This site follows the European public record, so the study is not listed under open trials. EU trial 2025-525111-17-00. No U.S. study number was posted. Company page.

Not for someone who has no symptoms yet. Adults who already have SCA3.

The latest study status will appear here.

Middle studies

None of the programs reviewed on 5 October 2026 were in a middle-stage study on their own. VO659 and S233107 are early studies and are listed above.

Large studies

None were still in a large final-stage study. Troriluzole had finished that stage and is listed under Stopped.

Asked a regulator

Stemchymal

A company in Japan has asked regulators to approve a stem-cell treatment for SCA3 and SCA6 (filed June 24, 2026). Asking isn’t the same as approval. It is not an approval in Japan, the United States, or Europe unless a regulator grants one.

Company announcement, 24 June 2026.

There isn’t a public trial listing for the application itself.

Approved

None. No drug on this page is approved to stop or slow SCA3.

Stopped

BIIB132 (Biogen)

An experimental drug aimed at the SCA3 gene. The early study was stopped in 2023, because the company decided to stop. It is not a current treatment.

NCT05160558.

The latest study status will appear here.

Troriluzole (Biohaven)

A pill studied for ataxia, including SCA3. The large study finished. In November 2025 the U.S. FDA said the application did not prove the drug works. That is a no, not an approval.

NCT03701399. Company announcement.

The latest study status will appear here.

What this board leaves out

Device studies and stem-cell studies that were not in the review table are not given a stage here. Two of them, temporal interference stimulation (NCT07743866) and cerebellar deep-brain stimulation (NCT07752095), were not yet recruiting at the review. They are not gene silencing. If a registry still lists them as open, they appear on Open trials with the registry’s own words.
